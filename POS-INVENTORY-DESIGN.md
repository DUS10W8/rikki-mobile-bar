## What the system must answer

Keep Square as the payment system and use Rikki’s app for menu, order routing, recipes, and inventory.

## Data and accounting

| Record | Required data and invariants |
| --- | --- |
| Inventory SKU and pack | Stable ID, display name, dimension, base unit, supplier pack factors, count method, optional cost. Use fixed-point liquid volume and mass or exact decimal quantities with an explicit rounding rule. Use integer each for indivisible items. Distinguish US fluid ounces from weight ounces. Keep money in integer cents and retain precision for fractional unit costs. |
| Recipe version and modifier | Immutable published ingredient quantities, yield, item references, and modifier operations with allowed combinations. Resolve substitutions on the server. Freeze resolved ingredients, selected modifiers, recipe version and sell price on each order line. |
| Event and inventory location | Link to the existing event record where appropriate. Identify commissary, reserve, and service stock. Record business timezone, shift, operator, and both occurred and recorded timestamps. |
| Order, line, fulfillment, tender | Stable IDs, event, source, quantity, and immutable price snapshot. Store payment references and status separately from preparation and handoff. Allow partial preparation of a multi-quantity line. |
| Stock transaction and entries | Append-only signed deltas by SKU and location with reason, source ID, idempotency key and actor. Support receiving, transfer, batch conversion, prepared drink, waste, physical return, and approved count adjustment. Write all entries for a transaction together. |
| Count session | Blind count by SKU/location at an explicit ledger cutoff. Record quantity, method and precision, counter, recount, expected quantity, and approval. Compute the variance before posting any adjustment and retain the original observations. |
| Batch or keg lot | Source inventory or supplier reference, produced/opened time, measured yield, location, remaining quantity and optional discard time. Use named lots when stock must remain distinguishable. |

**Prep is a conversion.** Juicing limes consumes whole fruit and creates measured lime juice. Cutting garnish consumes whole fruit and creates a counted wedge SKU. Record the real yields. A premixed Margarita base consumes raw inputs at prep and creates measured batch inventory. The line for a batched Margarita subsequently consumes the batch portion and ingredients added at service. Assign the cost of actual inputs across usable output, and avoid adding the same cost again through raw ingredient depletion at service. Compare actual batch yield to the prep standard separately from bar variance. If Patrón is an upgrade, batch the non-spirit base or maintain separately labeled batches for each tequila. Add Paloma carbonation at service under the pilot build.

**Deplete at the first irreversible preparation or handout.** The bartender action “Made” commits consumption for cocktails. For a sealed can, handout commits consumption when it leaves stock. A direct walk-up can combine this with its handoff action. For paid waiting orders, a short-lived reservation can reduce estimated availability without changing on-hand. Release it on cancellation or convert it atomically on preparation. Start with a manager-controlled sold-out toggle and projected stock warnings until counts and concurrency behavior are proven.

| Situation | Inventory result |
| --- | --- |
| Paid, cancelled before prep | Release any reservation. Zero consumption. Handle refund in Square. |
| Refunded after prep or service | Preserve the original depletion. Record the financial refund and product disposition independently. |
| Spilled after “Made,” then remade | Classify the original serving as waste and post a new preparation for the replacement. Count the original depletion once. |
| Spill while making a drink | Record the actual ingredients lost or a clearly labeled estimate. Post a separate preparation for the successful drink. |
| Comp, sample, or staff drink | Capture reason and actor, prepare from a real recipe, and show depletion even when revenue is zero. |
| Sealed item physically recovered and verified sellable | Post a linked return to stock once. A refund by itself cannot trigger it. |
| Stock moved to the trailer or back to the commissary | Same SKU debited and credited atomically. Report location balance and event-attributed consumption separately. |
| Physical count differs from expected | Keep the observed and expected quantities. Require reason and review for an adjustment, then append the correction. |

**Closeout math.** Compute expected close from the opening balance plus signed movements through the count cutoff. Show physical close minus expected close as variance; a negative value is a shortage. Show the pre-adjustment variance permanently and label estimated measurements. Example: 1,000 mL opening, 600 mL prepared, 50 mL of additional logged spills, no transfers, and 320 mL counted. Expected close is 350 mL, actual usage is 680 mL, and shortage is 30 mL. For event COGS, show standard recipe cost, tracked product cost including yield and waste, and approved variance value separately. Keep tips, tax, discounts, and refunds distinct from product revenue and inventory cost.

## Service and closeout

1. **Open.** Manager selects the event and published menu, records receiving or transfers, prepares batches with real yields, counts station stock, and verifies prices and default modifiers. Assign named roles for cashier, bartender, and manager.
2. **Ring.** Cashier sees item tiles, taps an allowed modifier, and sends a ticket tied to a stable order ID. The till and bartender share the same item names and event. The bartender sees only build instructions and exceptions needed to make the order. Put waste and remake within easy reach at the bar.
3. **Run.** Show event and station, active tickets, sync status, and low-stock items. Let the manager mark a product sold out and propose an approved substitute with its own recipe and availability. Capture unknown or unmapped Square items in exceptions instead of guessing a recipe.
4. **Recover.** When connectivity drops, use numbered backup tickets for made drinks, substitutions, comps, waste, and tender references. Reconcile by ticket number when connectivity returns and use stable operation IDs for retries. Treat pending or unknown card payments as unresolved and verify through Square before retrying. Scope any native offline mode to devices and payment configurations actually tested.
5. **Close.** Pause activity at each counted location or record a cutoff with intervening movements. Count bottles by sealed units plus measured partials, open mixers by usable remaining volume or case-specific policy, and packages by each. If weighing spirits, calibrate tare with the same bottle and attachments and establish a product-specific weight-to-volume method. Recount major differences, reconcile Square totals, resolve uncategorized movements, record leftovers transferred or discarded, and export an event report. Later corrections require actor and reason and create a new report revision.

## Integration with the current app

- The current event menu in [src/order/eventMenu.json](src/order/eventMenu.json) is Hamilton-specific. Retain it for existing orders and introduce a separate published event menu. The existing events table in [the command-center migration](supabase/migrations/20261026_command_center.sql) is the first candidate for event identity.
- [QR checkout](api/drink-checkout.js) builds Square line items from selected menu items and verifies completed payment before release. Keep its legacy orders working. Add structured event and order-line references for new events, with server-side prices and recipe snapshots.
- [Walk-up checkout](api/walkup-checkout.js) and [its release function](supabase/migrations/20261107000002_walkup_orders.sql) currently release a text drink ticket after verifying the payment. Extend this to a structured cart so modifiers and quantities stay attached to each drink. Start with QR and in-app walk-up. If staff ring directly in Square, test how its actual order lines and modifiers come through, map stable catalog IDs, and provide a reconciliation import before counting that channel as covered.
- [Bartender queue](src/bartender/BartenderPage.tsx) already supports preparation and handoff. Route the inventory-aware “Made” transition through an authenticated server/database command that locks the line, validates remaining quantity and event access, and commits the fulfillment and all corresponding stock entries in one transaction. Retrying the same operation returns the recorded result. Reject reuse of a key with a different payload. Revoke any client write path that could bypass this transition for inventory-enabled orders.
- Square remains authoritative for tenders and refunds. If webhook ingestion is added, verify signatures, durably capture each event ID, and deduplicate business effects separately by payment, order, or fulfillment identity. Reconcile missed and out-of-order notifications against current Square state. See [Square webhooks](https://developer.squareup.com/docs/webhooks/overview) and [Square Orders API](https://developer.squareup.com/reference/square/orders/webhooks).
- Keep privileged keys server-side. Scope writes to event roles with database authorization. Staff can ring or fulfill, leads can record waste, and managers can publish recipes, approve count adjustments, and close shifts. Limit customer information in logs and exports. Use reversible additive migrations and an event-level rollout switch. Historic tickets without recipe evidence remain visible without fabricated inventory entries.
